▍ 1.0 Three Concepts — Quick Clarification

Before we begin, let's quickly distinguish three terms that are often confused:

ConceptFormExample
Subject 话题A word or short phrase“Thinking”
Main Idea 主旨A complete sentence“Golding reveals that most people mistake feeling for thought, while genuine thinking is rare and demanding.”
Theme 主题A noun phrase (often a pair of opposing concepts)“Genuine thinking vs. pseudo‑thinking”

💡 Key rule: If your theme is a complete sentence, you're probably writing the Main Idea. A theme should be a noun phrase — ideally a pair of opposing concepts, because literature is built on conflict and tension.

Read each statement and decide whether it is a Subject, Main Idea, or Theme.

▍ 1.2 The Main Idea of This Essay (Review)

From our previous study, the Main Idea is:

“Golding reveals that most people mistake feeling and prejudice for thought, while genuine thinking is rare and demanding. He concludes, with quiet understatement, that true thinking is a serious lifelong commitment — not a casual hobby.”

This is the author's specific message in this essay. It contains Golding's judgment about the subject.

Now we move from what Golding says (Main Idea) to what human experience the text explores (Theme).

Step 4 — Formulate the theme as a paired phrase

“Genuine thinking vs. pseudo‑thinking”

More fully expressed:

“The scarcity of authentic intellectual engagement and the human tendency to mistake feeling, prejudice, and habit for thought.”

Supporting themes (secondary):

  • Appearance vs. reality in intellectual life 表象与现实
  • Conformity vs. intellectual independence 从众 vs. 独立思考
  • Self‑deception vs. self‑awareness 自我欺骗 vs. 自我认知
