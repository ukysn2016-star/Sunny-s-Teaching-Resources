Thinking as a Hobby

William Golding · Para 1–5

Para 1While I was still a boy, I came to the conclusion that there were three grades of thinking; and that I myself could not think at all.

Para 2It was the headmaster of my grammar school who first brought the subject of thinking before me. He had some statuettes in his study. They stood on a high cupboard behind his desk. One was a lady wearing nothing but a bath towel. She seemed frozen in an eternal panic lest the bath towel slip down any farther; and since she had no arms, she was in an unfortunate position to pull the towel up again. Next to her, crouched the statuette of a leopard, ready to spring down at the top drawer of a filing cabinet. Beyond the leopard was a naked, muscular gentleman, who sat, looking down, with his chin on his fist and his elbow on his knee. He seemed utterly miserable.

Para 3Some time later, I learned about these statuettes. The headmaster had placed them where they would face delinquent children, because they symbolized to him the whole of life. The naked lady was Venus. She was Love. She was not worried about the towel. She was just busy being beautiful. The leopard was Nature, and he was being natural. The muscular gentleman was not miserable. He was Rodin's Thinker, an image of pure thought.

Para 4I had better explain that I was a frequent visitor to the headmaster's study, because of the latest thing I had done or left undone. As we now say, I was not integrated. I was, if anything, disintegrated. Whenever I found myself in a penal position before the headmaster's desk I would sink my head, and writhe one shoe over the other.

Para 5–12

5. The headmaster would look at me and say,
6. “What are we going to do with you?”
7. Well, what were they going to do with me? I would writhe my shoe some more and stare down at the worn rug.
8. “Look up, boy! Can't you look up?”
9. Then I would look up at the cupboard, where the naked lady was frozen in her panic and the muscular gentleman contemplated the hindquarters of the leopard in endless gloom. I had nothing to say to the headmaster. His spectacles caught the light so that you could see nothing human behind them. There was no possibility of communication.
10. “Don't you ever think at all?”
11. No, I didn't think, wasn't thinking, couldn't think—I was simply waiting in anguish for the interview to stop.
12. “Then you'd better learn—hadn't you?”

修辞手法Irony · Simile · Metaphor · Metonymy · Synecdoche · Parallelism
Irony（反讽）
Irony is a gap between what is said and what is meant, or between what is expected and what actually happens. In verbal irony the speaker's words carry a meaning opposite to their literal sense.
反讽指“所言”与“所指”之间、或“预期”与“结果”之间的落差。言语反讽中，说话人的字面意思与实际含义相反。
I was not integrated. I was, if anything, disintegrated.
我这个人没有被整合起来。要说有什么的话，我是被拆解开来的。（Para 4）
赏析 这是典型的自嘲式反讽（self-deprecating irony）。integrated 是当时教育心理学的时髦词，指“人格健全、适应良好”；叙述者先用否定句承认自己不符合标准，随即用 disintegrated（分崩离析）把这一诊断推向荒诞的极端。表面自我贬低，实则讽刺那套动辄给学生贴标签的评判体系。
补充 同段还可见 Para 16 “With a modest satisfaction, he would tell me that he had thought a bit himself.”（自得的反讽）与 Para 32 把“大型企业、中央集权政府、军队、婚姻”称作 a number of trifles（低调陈述式反讽）。
Metaphor（隐喻）
A metaphor states that one thing is another, transferring the qualities of the second onto the first without using like or as.
隐喻直接把甲说成乙，不使用 like / as，从而把乙的属性投射到甲身上。
The naked lady was Venus. She was Love. … The leopard was Nature, and he was being natural. The muscular gentleman was not miserable. He was Rodin's Thinker, an image of pure thought.
裸体的女士是维纳斯。她就是爱。……豹子是自然，而他正在表现得自然而然。肌肉发达的绅士并不痛苦。他是罗丹的《思想者》，纯粹思想的形象。（Para 3）
赏析 一连串判断句 She was Love / The leopard was Nature / He was Rodin's Thinker 构成并列隐喻：三座石膏小像被直接等同于“爱”“自然”“纯粹思想”三个抽象概念。隐喻是全篇的思想支架——后文的三个思想等级，正是从这三座雕像的寓意中生长出来的。
补充 同段并列结构 She was Love / The leopard was Nature / He was Rodin's Thinker 亦可视为 Parallelism（平行结构），两种手法在此叠加。
Synecdoche（提喻）
Synecdoche is a figure of speech in which a part stands for the whole, or the whole for a part.
提喻是以部分代整体、或以整体代部分的修辞手法。
Whenever I found myself in a penal position before the headmaster's desk I would sink my head, and writhe one shoe over the other.
每当我站在校长书桌前那个受罚的位置上，我就会低下头，一只鞋在另一只鞋上蹭来蹭去。（Para 4）
赏析 “一只鞋蹭另一只鞋”只是脚上的小动作，却被用来指代整个身体的局促与全副心思的窘迫——以局部动作写全局心理。后文 Para 20–22 用 the neck（脖子）代 Houghton 本人及其被本能支配的行为（Mr Houghton thought with his neck），是同一手法的延伸。
补充 Para 9 “the muscular gentleman contemplated the hindquarters of the leopard”以 hindquarters（后半身）代雕像整体，也是提喻。
Metonymy（转喻）
Metonymy replaces a word with another closely associated with it — the container for the contained, the symbol for the thing symbolized, the institution for its members.
转喻是用与本体密切相关的另一名称来代替本体（容器代内容、符号代本体、机构代成员等）。
The naked lady was Venus… The leopard was Nature… The muscular gentleman was not miserable. He was Rodin's Thinker, an image of pure thought.
裸体的女士是维纳斯……豹子是自然……肌肉发达的绅士并不痛苦。他是罗丹的《思想者》，纯粹思想的形象。（Para 3）
赏析 以 Venus 代 Love、以 Rodin's Thinker 代 Pure Thought，是用承载体（神话人物／艺术品名称）代替抽象概念。它与上一条 Metaphor 相邻而不同：隐喻建立在相似之上，转喻建立在关联之上。同一组句子，从“像什么”看是隐喻，从“用什么名称代替”看是转喻。
补充 全文另见 Para 21 “He would bang the desk, his neck would bulge still further and go red.”（以 neck 代 Houghton 其人）；Para 23 “its neck is being remorselessly twisted toward a skirt”以 skirt（裙子）代“路过的女性”。
Simile（明喻） 全文版锚点
A simile makes an explicit comparison between two unlike things, usually introduced by like or as.
明喻用 like / as 等词明确地把两种不同事物作比。
Man enjoys agreement as cows will graze all the same way on the side of a hill.
人喜欢一致，就像牛总是朝着山坡的同一侧吃草。（Para 24）
赏析 把“人云亦云的群氓”比作“朝同一方向吃草的牛群”，贬义鲜明，是 Para 24 讽刺的高潮。
补充 全文版锚点：Para 23 “as proficient as most businessmen's golf, as honest as most politicians' intentions, or as coherent as most books that get written”；Para 31 “like so many rotten apples off a tree”。
Parallelism（平行结构） 全文版锚点
Parallelism is the use of identical or similar grammatical structures in successive phrases, clauses or sentences to give balance, rhythm and emphasis.
平行结构指在连续的短语、分句或句子中使用相同或相近的语法结构，以取得平衡、节奏与强调。
To hear people justify their habit of hunting foxes… To hear our Prime Minister talk about the great benefit we conferred on India… To hear American politicians talk about peace and refuse to join the League of Nations.
听人们为猎狐的陋习狡辩……听我们的首相大谈我们给印度带来多大恩惠……听美国政客嘴上高呼和平却拒绝加入国际联盟。（Para 25）
赏析 三个 To hear… 不定式短语排比而下，语气层层递进，把“二等思考”带来的快感写成一串连锁的荒诞，讽刺力度随结构叠加而增强。
补充 全文版锚点：Para 23 “as proficient as…, as honest as…, or as coherent as…”；Para 34 “where prejudices are called loyalties, pointless actions are turned into customs by repetition”。
